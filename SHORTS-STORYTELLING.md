# Hold Up — Shorts Storytelling

Supplement to the original SKILL.md. Target 30–45 seconds (a soft target: never trade clarity or quality for brevity); this replaces SKILL.md's 55–70 s. Preserve verified facts and the established art style.

## Script pattern
1. **0–3 seconds: Hook.** Start with a specific surprising event, danger, contradiction or question. The first image should prove the premise visually. No greeting or logo.
2. **3–8 seconds: Orient.** Give only the context necessary to understand who, where and what is at stake.
3. **Middle: Escalate.** Use a chain of concrete cause → action → consequence. Add a new fact, complication or visual reveal every beat; don't repeat what is already visible.
4. **Late beat: Mechanism or reversal.** Explain the surprising reason in plain English, ideally with a simple visual analogy.
5. **Ending: Payoff.** Finish on the consequence, twist, ironic callback or satisfying answer. No filler outro.

These are creative defaults, not fixed second-by-second rules. Fit the actual narration and story.

## What tends to work creatively
- A clear physical problem the audience can visualize
- Specific stakes, scale and consequences
- A curiosity gap that the video actually closes
- One surprising fact per beat instead of a dense fact dump
- Expressive reactions and visual comedy where appropriate
- A strong final image that reinforces the ending
- Accurate, accessible language

## Avoid
- Generic setup: 'Have you ever wondered...'
- Clickbait promises the story cannot support
- Long dates/names before viewers know why they matter
- Repeating the same information in voice, captions and picture
- Fake jeopardy, invented quotes or fabricated historical details
- Overexplaining after the payoff
- Treating a formula as a guarantee of views

## Accuracy
For true stories, maintain a source-backed fact sheet. Mark unconfirmed details as unconfirmed; never animate speculation as established fact. Separate dramatization from documented actions.

## Narration
Write for spoken rhythm, not reading. Short sentences, concrete verbs, natural emphasis. Record the approved voice with the existing workflow; align visuals to actual word timings. Do not change the established narrator without permission.

## Learning from uploads
After each release, record topic, hook, runtime, title, views after a consistent period, viewed-vs-swiped, average view duration, retention curve, completion, likes/comments/shares and any obvious distribution caveats. Compare similar videos; distinguish observations from hypotheses. Change one or two variables at a time. Don't rewrite production rules based on one upload.
