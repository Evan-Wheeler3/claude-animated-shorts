# Hold Up — Shorts Storytelling

Supplement to the original SKILL.md. Target 45–60 seconds (a soft target: the story decides; never pad and never trade clarity for brevity). Evidence: `research/vasa/`. Preserve verified facts and the established art style.

## Script pattern
1. **0–3 seconds: Hook.** Start with a specific surprising event, danger, contradiction or question. The first image should prove the premise visually. No greeting or logo.
2. **3–8 seconds: Orient.** Give only the context necessary to understand who, where and what is at stake.
3. **Middle: Escalate.** Use a chain of concrete cause → action → consequence. Add a new fact, complication or visual reveal every beat; don't repeat what is already visible.
4. **Late beat: Mechanism or reversal.** Explain the surprising reason in plain English, ideally with a simple visual analogy.
5. **Ending: Payoff.** Finish on the consequence, twist, ironic callback or satisfying answer. No filler outro.

These are creative defaults, not fixed second-by-second rules. Fit the actual narration and story.

## Preferred structure for "why is it like that" topics: the Fix-Fail Chain (owner pick, 2026-11-26)
Owner shared a competitor script (tyres, ~170 words) as the style to beat. We copy the STRUCTURE, never the topic,
lines or beats of someone else's video. Pattern:
1. **Start with the simplest version, in second person:** "If you ... , you've built a ...". No dates, no names.
2. **Chain:** each line = one fix ("So, you ...") + the new problem it causes ("but ..."). 6-10 links, each a concrete,
   visual failure (it slices, it shreds, it floats, it tears). A new picture every link (~2-3 s).
3. **Drop the title's answer in the middle** ("That's why X is Y, but ..."): the video keeps paying off after it.
4. **End on a reframe** (a surprising true consequence of the final design) **+ a callback** to the first line.
5. ~150-180 words; every number must be sourced (no "a bucket every third of a second" unless we can cite it).

## What tends to work creatively
- A clear physical problem the audience can visualize
- Specific stakes, scale and consequences
- A curiosity gap that the video actually closes
- One surprising fact per beat instead of a dense fact dump
- Expressive reactions and visual comedy where appropriate
- A strong final image that reinforces the ending
- Accurate, accessible language

## Avoid
- Generic setup: 'Have you ever wondered...'
- Clickbait promises the story cannot support
- Long dates/names before viewers know why they matter
- Repeating the same information in voice, captions and picture
- Fake jeopardy, invented quotes or fabricated historical details
- Overexplaining after the payoff
- Treating a formula as a guarantee of views

## Accuracy
For true stories, maintain a source-backed fact sheet. Mark unconfirmed details as unconfirmed; never animate speculation as established fact. Separate dramatization from documented actions.

## Narration (written for the ear: the owner rewrote the first Vasa draft for breaking these)
Write for spoken rhythm, not reading. Record the approved voice with the existing workflow; align visuals to actual
word timings. Do not change the established narrator without permission.
- **Full sentences only.** No telegraph fragments ("Four sails up. A light breeze. Then one gust."): heard aloud they
  sound like a list of disconnected images. Each sentence says who did what.
- **No contractions** ("it's", "she'd", "Vasa's" possessives are fine only in names the owner wants). TTS swallows
  them and listeners lose the word.
- **Plain words for jargon.** Say what a thing is ("cannon hatches", not "gunports"); a technical term needs its
  everyday meaning in the same sentence.
- **Cause, then effect, in order.** Every beat names its cause: "The cannon hatches opened for a salute. Just then, a
  strong gust hit." Never make the viewer infer the link.
- **Hook = one bold, concrete claim with a number** ("Sweden's largest warship only sailed for twenty minutes.").
  Propose the boldest defensible version; flag risk once; the owner decides.
- **Mark emphasis while writing** in `voice/SCRIPT-emphasis.md`: 1-3 `*stressed*` words on lines that land a point
  (numbers, the twist word, the punchline). End punch lines with "!".
- **Read it aloud before approval:** the owner approves the recorded narration with the music bed, not text.

## Learning from uploads
After each release, record topic, hook, runtime, title, views after a consistent period, viewed-vs-swiped, average view duration, retention curve, completion, likes/comments/shares and any obvious distribution caveats. Compare similar videos; distinguish observations from hypotheses. Change one or two variables at a time. Don't rewrite production rules based on one upload.
