# Hold Up — Camera Direction

Supplement to SKILL.md, not a replacement. Pacing targets (how often the picture changes) are in `references/reports/editing.md`. Use the existing Remotion/Three.js camera keys, DOF, ink and transition systems. The goal is more expressive direction, not constant motion.

## Every shot needs a reason
For each narration beat, choose the **subject**, **emotion or information**, **shot size**, **camera action**, and **visual payoff**. Default to the smallest shot that communicates the beat clearly on a phone.

## Shot vocabulary
- **Extreme close-up / insert:** tiny mechanism, hand action, crucial object, or comic facial detail. Reveal the thing the audience must notice.
- **Close-up:** reaction, realization, fear, embarrassment, punchline. Frame eyes and expression prominently.
- **Medium:** character interacting with an object, conversation, physical comedy.
- **Wide:** geography, scale, cause-and-effect, aftermath. Don't use as the default for every line.
- **Overhead / high angle:** show routes, collision paths, spatial relationships or a visual comparison.
- **Low angle:** make a threat or machine feel enormous.
- **Tracking / follow:** move with the important object or character; maintain screen direction.
- **Push-in:** emphasize a reveal, decision or rising danger; use a quick punch-in sparingly for jokes.
- **Whip pan / match move:** transition between causally connected subjects when spatially clear.
- **Hold:** deliberate suspense or deadpan humor. Stillness can outperform movement, but a hold is a slow creeping push (0.15-0.35 %/f), never a frozen frame; a true freeze only as a named comic beat in the shot list.

## Directing rules
1. Hook begins with the strongest visual event, not an establishing shot or logo. Then orient the viewer.
2. For each change of subject, ask whether a change in scale or angle clarifies it.
3. Show a reaction in close-up when the reaction **is** the joke or story beat.
4. On impact, show anticipation → contact → consequence. A brief hold or silence before impact can add weight.
5. Use 3D space: foreground occlusion, parallax, motivated orbit, overhead geography, low-angle scale. Never orbit just to show off.
6. Preserve screen direction, eyelines, object placement and geography between cuts.
7. Don't confuse motion with engagement. Avoid perpetual zooms, random shakes, excessive whip pans, or constant wide/full-body shots.
8. Keep the subject inside the vertical safe area; review shots at phone size.
9. Sync the visual payoff with the corresponding spoken word; existing SKILL.md guidance allows 0–3 frames of lead.
10. Start with the existing camera system and look values; change shot selection and blocking before changing rendering technology.

## Shot-plan contract
For each shot, specify:
- Time or aligned narration words
- Narrative purpose / emotion
- Main subject and action
- Framing (ECU/CU/MS/WS/overhead/low angle)
- Camera start and end positions or movement
- Foreground/background layers and focus target
- Transition and sound beat
- Continuity constraints
- One frame that should be visually memorable

## Review pass
Watch muted at phone size. Can a stranger follow the physical action? Is the most important expression large enough? Are multiple consecutive shots flat wides? Does every move help comprehension or emotion? Fix weak framing before adding effects.
