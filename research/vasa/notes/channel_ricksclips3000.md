## 2. Topics, Shorts vs long-form mix, runtime, cadence

### Takeaway
The channel posts animated "true story" / history / "why X" Shorts. The style is flat 2D cartoon characters in 3D environments with one narrator. The long-form mix, the channel's real runtimes and its posting cadence could not be verified.

### Cited Findings
- Revid describes the format as "true story cartoon shorts" and attributes its popularity to @RicksClips3000. — [Revid.ai](https://www.revid.ai/true-story-cartoon-shorts)
- An open-source recreation describes the style as "3D cartoon true-story Shorts" with "flat inked characters acting in a detailed three.js world, one flowing camera, voice, captions, a full sound track and a colour grade". This is a recreation, not the channel's own statement. — [GitHub shelohermituss-hub/Ricksclip-](https://github.com/shelohermituss-hub/Ricksclip-)
- A third-party reconstruction (Dumble on X) gives 1080x1920, 30 fps, about **65 s**, one voice, flat 2D characters in 3D sets, a **new shot about every 2 s**, and 1–2-word captions. The author says outright that he does not know the real pipeline. — [Dumble on X](https://x.com/Y0urDumble/status/2115164938884862375)
- Some tutorial titles call the style "stickman" animation. — [YouTube: Make Viral Stickman Shorts… like Ricks Clips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M_1IGXSQX_g)

### Inferences
- With about 150 videos and a breakout around October 2026, cadence is probably high (daily or near-daily) recently. This is UNVERIFIED: no upload dates were seen.
- The ~65 s runtime is one imitator's estimate. It suggests the channel's real Shorts may run longer than the 30–45 s target. This is UNVERIFIED.

### Gaps
- Shorts vs long-form split, the channel's actual runtimes, and videos per week over recent months: could not access the /shorts and /videos tabs or stats pages.

## 5. Script structure and hook patterns

### Takeaway
No first-party information exists. Every structural description comes from imitators reverse-engineering the style: a promise in the first line paid off in the last line, a hook within about 2 s, a hook → complication → escalation → reveal → close arc, fast cuts and word-by-word captions.

### Cited Findings
- The first line makes a promise and the last line pays it off. 1–2-word captions, a cut about every 2 s. — [Dumble on X](https://x.com/Y0urDumble/status/2115164938884862375)
- A five-block structure: **hook (first 2 s) → complication → escalation → reveal → close**, with captions of up to 4 words and the spoken word highlighted. — [GitHub MikelFR17/claude-animation-skills PR #2](https://github.com/MikelFR17/claude-animation-skills/pull/2)
- Hook types common in top Shorts are fail, reaction, cold open and impact. A Ricks Clips video is the example given for a "fail" hook. — [viralhooks.org](https://viralhooks.org/create/most-viewed-youtube-shorts/)
- A tutorial summary says its creator had Claude study 20 reference shorts to learn the style. That was the imitator's workflow, not the channel's. — [Revid.ai summary via search](https://www.revid.ai/true-story-cartoon-shorts)

### Inferences
- For Vasa, a "fail" cold-open hook (the ship sinking on its maiden voyage) followed by the "why" fits the reported pattern.
