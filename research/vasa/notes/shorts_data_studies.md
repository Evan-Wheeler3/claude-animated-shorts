# Short-form video retention and engagement: what the data says (2023-2026)

Scope: third-party data and studies on YouTube Shorts (TikTok, Reels or Douyin where Shorts data is missing) about hooks, length, pacing, narration, captions, loops and educational features. The goal is editing and script rules for 30-45 s animated history Shorts.

**How this was researched (read first).** The tool that fetches full web pages could not reach any outside site in this session (opus.pro, quso.ai and marketing-trends-congress.com all failed DNS). Every finding below comes from search-engine result summaries and snippets, not from reading the primary documents myself. Sample sizes and figures are as reported in those summaries. Check the primary source before quoting any number publicly. Allowing those hosts in the session's network settings would let someone verify the details.

**Evidence grades used:**
- **strong**: peer-reviewed or large-sample work with a stated method
- **moderate**: a large vendor dataset with a stated sample but a thin method, or a small academic study
- **weak**: vendor claims with no method, or secondary restatements
- **anecdote**: one creator, or guru lore

Each finding is also tagged **(a)** large-sample data study, **(b)** controlled experiment or academic research, or **(c)** anecdote or lore.

## 1. Optimal Shorts length and its relationship to views and retention

### Takeaway
The measured data conflicts, and the outcome you measure decides the answer:
- Median-views analyses favour very short Shorts (11-20 s).
- Total-view and engagement analyses favour longer ones (41-60 s).
- Academic short-video work (Douyin) points to an optimum of about 30-60 s.

No dataset isolates length from content quality. A 30-45 s target is not contradicted by any data, and it sits inside the academic "optimum window".

### Cited Findings
- **(a) quso.ai, 108,000+ "matured" Shorts.** Median views peak at 11-20 s (median 901 views) and fall off in both directions. The analysis uses medians so viral outliers don't distort it. Method details, date range and niche mix could not be verified. Grade: moderate (large n, vendor, method unclear). [quso.ai](https://quso.ai/research/youtube-shorts-length)
- **(a) Paddy Galloway and Chris Gileta (2023), 5,400 Shorts from 33 channels.** Longer Shorts performed better on average, and 50-60 s Shorts got the most views. The authors caution that the findings "hold true for the sample... may not be true for yours". Averages are skewed by viral hits, and 33 channels is a small cluster sample. Grade: moderate-weak. [Adobe/vidIQ summary page](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/youtube-shorts-length-study); also [Tubefilter coverage](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/12/07/cracking-the-code-youtube-shorts/amp/)
- **(a) Adobe Express using vidIQ data, April 2024, 1,000 Shorts from the top 100 US channels** (data pulled 4-10 April 2024). Shorts longer than 41 s got 31% more engagement on average. This measures engagement, not views or retention. Grade: moderate-weak (top channels only, averages). [Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/youtube-shorts-length-study); [ContentGrip summary](https://www.contentgrip.com/youtube-shorts-length-study/)
- **(c) Adobe Express creator survey, June 2026.** 53% of creators named under 30 s as their top-performing format for views. This is creator belief, not measured performance. Grade: anecdote (survey of opinions). [Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/youtube-shorts-length-study)
- **(a) Metricool (via Statista):** the average TikTok was 42.7 s in 2024, up from 39 s in 2023. **(a) A later Metricool figure** put the average TikTok at 41 s, with viewers watching only 3.75 s on average and just 4% watched in full. These describe the market, not a length optimum. Grade: moderate. [Vidico roundup](https://vidico.com/news/short-form-video-statistics/); [Metricool short-video report](https://metricool.com/short-video-report/)
- **(a) Metricool 2025 State of Short-Form Video, 5,696,685 videos from 582,456 accounts.** TikTok has the highest average engagement rate (8.24%). Metricool 2026 reports that TikTok gets up to 42% more views than Reels and more than 6 times more than YouTube Shorts. Grade: moderate (very large n; engagement definitions are vendor-specific). [Marketing Tech News](https://www.marketingtechnews.net/news/what-the-data-tells-us-about-short-form-video-insights-from-metricools-2025-state-of-short-form-video-report/); [Metricool](https://metricool.com/short-video-report/)
- **(a) Emplifi 2023, brand accounts.** Longer videos got more views on Instagram, while TikTok views "remained in the same range regardless of video length". Grade: moderate-weak (brands only). [Emplifi press release](https://emplifi.io/press/emplifi-reports-instagram-reels-outperformed-other-video-content-instagram-facebook-tiktok/)
- **(a) Socialinsider, about 69 million videos.** Engagement by views is 2.60% on TikTok, 0.45% on Reels and 0.30% on YouTube Shorts. Another roundup quotes Socialinsider 2026 at 4.20% for TikTok. The figures conflict because of different denominators (views vs followers). Grade: moderate for scale, weak for comparability. [Vidico roundup](https://vidico.com/news/short-form-video-statistics/)
- **(b) Marketing Trends Congress 2025 paper, "How Video Length Shapes Engagement".** It reports the optimal short-form length for engagement as 34.69 s. I could not retrieve the authors, platform or sample size. Grade: weak until verified (academic conference, details unknown). [PDF](https://archives.marketing-trends-congress.com/2025/pages/PDF/060.pdf)
- **(b) PLOS One / PMC, 4,422 Douyin videos from 18 science and technology journals** (multiple regression, Elaboration Likelihood Model framework):
  - Duration and visual quality correlate positively with how widely the videos spread.
  - "30 seconds to 1 minute" is the optimal duration "balancing information density and cognitive load".
  - Subtitles and clear covers have stable positive effects.

  Grade: moderate (peer-reviewed, cross-sectional, niche). [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12854476/); [PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?type=printable&id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0341716). These two links are the same paper, not independent evidence.
- **(b) TikTok/Douyin videos on age-related macular degeneration (cross-sectional).** Duration correlated mildly and positively with both quality and engagement (r = 0.23-0.30, P < 0.05). Grade: moderate-weak (small, health niche). [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12832813/)
- **(b) Recommender-systems research on Kuaishou and WeChat Channels data:**
  - Average view time rises almost linearly with video length.
  - Completion rate is biased toward short videos, because short videos are completed more often.

  Raw "% viewed" therefore naturally favours shorter clips, and platforms explicitly correct for this "duration bias". Grade: strong (peer-reviewed or arXiv on large production logs; some methods deployed on Kuaishou). [arXiv 2308.14276](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.14276); [arXiv 2206.06003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06003v1); [arXiv 2406.07932](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.07932). The DVR paper's dataset has about 96,418 videos and 7.3 million records: [arXiv 2208.05190](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.05190)

### Inferences
- The "short = better" and "long = better" camps are measuring different things: median views or percentage viewed against total views or watch time. Recommender systems on comparable platforms normalise for length, so a longer Short isn't penalised for a lower percentage viewed in the way raw dashboards suggest. This is inferred from Kuaishou research. YouTube's Shorts ranking internals aren't public.
- For 30-45 s educational content, the Douyin science-video study is the closest analogue and supports the range. The quso median-views result is the main counter-signal, and it is not niche-specific.

### Gaps
- No Tubefilter, TubeBuddy, OpusClip, HubSpot or Dash Hudson Shorts-length study with a stated method came up in search. OpusClip has blog posts ("Ideal YouTube Shorts length... data-backed") but the page could not be fetched to check sample or method.
- No study controls for content quality or topic when comparing length, so none of this is causal.
- YouTube itself publishes no length-vs-performance data.

## 2. Benchmarks for viewed-vs-swiped and average percentage viewed (APV)

### Takeaway
YouTube publishes **no official benchmark** for either metric. All published "good" thresholds are vendor rules of thumb. They cluster around 70-80%+ viewed (not swiped) and roughly 70-90% APV for Shorts under 35 s. Real channel baselines can be far lower: one documented channel has about 27% viewed. Benchmark against your own Shorts of similar length.

### Cited Findings
- **YouTube help (official definition):** "viewed vs swiped away" is the percentage of times viewers watched your Short instead of swiping away. No target is given. Grade: strong as a definition. [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281)
- **1of10 tiered table:** for viewed vs swiped, 70-79% is middle and 80%+ is top. For APV on Shorts under 35 s, 80-90% is middle and 90%+ is top. The same source says outright that YouTube publishes no official benchmarks and that its tables come from creator-reported data. Grade: weak. [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics/)
- **Vendor guidance of 75-80% viewed** as the range that makes wider distribution "more likely". No data shown. Grade: weak. [ContentStudio](https://contentstudio.io/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics-guide); [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-rate)
- **Vendor 2026 framework:** above 80% at 3 s, above 60% at the midpoint, above 70% APV overall. Another vendor sets targets by length: more than 60% for under 15 s, more than 50% for 15-30 s, more than 40% for 30-60 s. Neither shows its method, and the two disagree. Grade: weak. [vidpal.ai](https://www.vidpal.ai/blog/good-retention-rate-for-youtube-shorts-benchmarks-2026); [gofaceless.ai](https://www.gofaceless.ai/en/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-rate-benchmarks)
- **Bytecap research page:** there is no universal threshold, so compare against your own Shorts of similar length, format and topic. Grade: weak but sensible. [Bytecap](https://www.bytecap.io/research/youtube-shorts-retention-benchmarks)
- **(c) Social Media Examiner creator example:** the channel's 90-day ratio is 26.6% viewed vs 73.4% swiped away, yet it still gets views. Grade: anecdote, but it shows real ratios can be far below the guru thresholds. [Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/how-to-analyze-your-youtube-shorts/)

### Inferences
- Because completion is duration-biased (see section 1), an APV target should scale with length. A 40 s Short hitting 70-80% APV is likely strong. Applying the "90%+" bar meant for under-35 s Shorts is unfounded.
- "Viewed vs swiped" depends on the first second or two plus the feed context, so it is the cleanest hook metric to A/B-compare between your own uploads.

### Gaps
- No large-sample published distribution (percentiles) of viewed-vs-swiped or APV across Shorts was found.
- YouTube has not published how these metrics weight into recommendations.

## 3. Hook and first 1-3 seconds: measured drop-off and hook types

### Takeaway
The only quantified retention curve found comes from a vendor (MuseOn, 10,000 videos). In that curve, about 16% of viewers are gone by 1 s, 44% by 3 s and 72% by 10 s. No peer-reviewed study was found that compares hook *types* (question, cold-open action, bold claim and so on) on organic Shorts. Popular hook-type "rules" and the widely repeated Meta and TikTok statistics have no traceable primary source.

### Cited Findings
- **(a) MuseOn, 10,000 authorised videos** (10-60 s, at least 1,000 views each). Median retention was 84% at 1 s, 56% at 3 s, 44% at 5 s and 28% at 10 s. A vendor dataset with a stated sample and inclusion rule, but the platform mix and method are not detailed. Grade: moderate-weak. [MuseOn](https://www.museon.ai/blog/hooked-or-skipped)
- **(c/weak) "65% of people who watch the first 3 s keep watching for at least 10 s, 45% for 30 s",** attributed to Facebook or Meta internal data. It is repeated widely with no primary link, and I could not trace the original. Grade: unverified lore. [Teleprompter blog](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/tiktok-3-second-rule); [go-viral.app](https://www.go-viral.app/blog/hook-first-3-seconds/)
- **(weak) "63% of the highest click-through videos hook viewers within the first 3 seconds",** attributed to TikTok for Business. It concerns ads and click-through, and the primary source was not located. "Strong hooks are 2.4x more likely to reach the For You page" (attributed to TikTok Creator Insights via Brandefy) is unverified. Grade: weak. [Hansen Commerce](https://hansencommerce.com/insights-tiktok-hook-3-seconds); [Teleprompter blog](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/tiktok-3-second-rule)
- **(weak) TikTok guidance reportedly says:** state the content proposition within 3 s and the hook within 6 s. Not confirmed against TikTok's own page. [Kompozy guide](https://kompozy.io/guides/short-form-video-hooks)
- **(b) "Decoding the Hook" (arXiv, 2026):** a multimodal LLM framework for analysing the first 3 s of *video ads*. Only a secondary summary was seen, and the findings were not verified. [Digimarcon summary](https://digimarcon.com/scroll-stopping-secrets-the-architecture-of-viral-video-hooks)
- **(c) Claims of "400 ms pre-screening" neuroscience** and "1-2 second swipe rate is YouTube's primary signal" have no cited evidence. Grade: lore. [Vidcognition](https://vidcognition.com/blog/first-3-seconds-video-hook-neuroscience)

### Inferences
- If MuseOn's curve is roughly representative, the first 3 s lose more viewers than seconds 3-10 combined (44% vs 28%). That supports prioritising the cold open over anything else in the edit, as a rule of thumb rather than a proven law.
- Hook-type advice ("open with a question", "open mid-action") is untested lore. A creator should A/B test on their own channel by comparing viewed-vs-swiped.

### Gaps
- No controlled experiment comparing hook types for organic short-form video was found.
- No YouTube-published Shorts retention curve was found.

## 4. Pacing: cut frequency and shot length

### Takeaway
There is strong academic data that Hollywood average shot length (ASL) fell from about 12-15 s (1930s-40s) to about 2.5-4 s (2000s-2010s). There are theoretical arguments, contested, that shot rhythms track attention. **No large-sample measurement of cut frequency in high-performing Shorts, TikToks or Reels, and no causal study linking cut rate to short-form retention, was found.** One small student study (TikTok) associates "fast pacing" with higher engagement.

### Cited Findings
- **(b) Cutting, DeLong, Brunick, Iricinschi and Candan, "Quicker, Faster, Darker" (i-Perception, 2011).** Average shot durations in Hollywood films fell from about 15 s to about 3.5 s over 75 years, and films also got darker. Grade: strong (systematic film-corpus measurement). [PMC3485803](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3485803)
- **(b) Other Cutting estimates:** about 12 s in 1930 to about 2.5 s today for English-language films. Another report says about 4 s now. Figures vary with sample. Grade: strong for the trend, moderate for exact values. [Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/index%2ephp/node/271563); [FlowingData](https://flowingdata.com/2014/09/22/evolution-of-movies/)
- **(b) Cutting, DeLong and Nothelfer (2010).** From about 1960 to 2005, fluctuations in shot duration approached a 1/f, fractal-like pattern that the authors relate to attention rhythms. Critics doubt the claimed link between attention processes and shot lengths. Grade: moderate (contested interpretation). [Cinemetrics commentary](https://cinemetrics.uchicago.edu/article/7026830d-73a7-4b27-b8fe-41ebe823589b)
- **(b) Eye-tracking work on dynamic scenes (Mital et al., 2011, cited in Cognitive Research review).** Cuts and motion force viewers to reorient attention. Gaze tends to cluster ("attentional synchrony") in edited film. This is not about short-form specifically. Grade: moderate. [Springer, Cognitive Research](https://rd.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s41235-018-0091-x.pdf)
- **(b, small) Sheynin, The Young Researcher (student journal), TikTok.** "Medium video length, fast pacing, present text overlay, vertical format, and music without lyrics maximized engagement". The author says length and pacing need further investigation. Grade: weak (small, student, non-peer-reviewed-level). [PDF](http://www.theyoungresearcher.com/papers/sheynin.pdf)

### Inferences
- A 2-4 s average shot length is the established mainstream-film norm. Matching or slightly beating it (for example a new visual every 1.5-3 s) is a reasonable *convention*, but no short-form data shows a specific cut rate raises retention.
- For animated explainer content, "visual change" (a camera move, a new element, a text pop) probably serves the same reorienting function as a hard cut (from the eye-tracking rationale). This is inference, not tested.

### Gaps
- No large-sample cut-frequency analysis of top Shorts was found. Any "cut every 2 seconds" rule is lore.
- No experiment on cut rate vs retention in vertical video was found.

## 5. Narration speech rate and comprehension

### Takeaway
There is strong evidence that comprehension holds up well above normal speaking rates: lecture video at up to 2x speed shows little loss, and loss is clear at 2.5x. Normal speech is about 150 wpm, and comprehension starts to decline at about 200-275 wpm for audio alone. In a 6.9-million-session MOOC study, faster-speaking instructors drew *more* engagement. Brisk narration of about 160-200 wpm is therefore supported for 30-45 s Shorts. Pushing much past about 250 wpm risks losing comprehension, especially for dense history content.

### Cited Findings
- **(a/b) Guo, Kim and Rubin (2014), "How video production affects student engagement", ACM Learning@Scale.** The study covers 6.9 million video-watching sessions across 4 edX courses. Engagement was measured as watch time and attempts at the follow-up problems.
  - Shorter videos are more engaging, with median engagement capped at about 6 minutes.
  - Students engaged more with fast-speaking instructors, who conveyed more energy and enthusiasm.

  The exact wpm bins were not verified in this session. Grade: strong (huge n, observational, MOOC context not Shorts). [Paper PDF (Rochester)](https://www.cs.rochester.edu/hci/pubs/pdfs/edX-MOOC-video-production-and-engagement_LAS-2014.pdf); [UMD summary](https://pm.umd.edu/2020/12/09/video-production-guidance/); [UW-Madison KB summary](https://kb.wisc.edu/remote-instruction/103763)
- **(b) Murphy, Hoover, Agadzhanyan, Kuehn and Castel (2021), Applied Cognitive Psychology.** Lecture video was shown at 1x, 1.5x, 2x and 2.5x, with immediate and one-week tests. Little effect until above 2x: the 2x group scored 25/40, normal speed about 26/40, and 2.5x about 22/40. Grade: strong (controlled experiment, college students). [UCLA newsroom](https://newsroom.ucla.edu/releases/learning-while-speed-watching-class-videos); [PDF](https://castel.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/111/2022/03/Applied-Cognitive-Psychology-2021-Murphy-Learning-in-double-time-The-effect-of-lecture-video-speed-on-immediate-and.pdf)
- **(b) Educational Psychology Review (2024), audio-only vs audio-visual at faster speeds.** Watching lecture videos at speeds up to 2x does not significantly affect performance. The multimodal nature of audio-visual material may protect comprehension. Grade: strong. [Springer s10648-024-09917-7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-024-09917-7)
- **(b) Baselines cited in that literature:** human speech is about 150 wpm (Peelle and Davis, 2012). Comprehension of audio-only speech declines at about 275 wpm (Foulke and Sticht, 1969). Another source puts the decline at 200-270 wpm. Grade: strong (classic literature, via secondary citation). [Murphy et al. PDF](https://castel.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/111/2022/03/Applied-Cognitive-Psychology-2021-Murphy-Learning-in-double-time-The-effect-of-lecture-video-speed-on-immediate-and.pdf)

### Inferences
- Visuals plus captions give multimodal support, which suggests narration somewhat faster than conversational speech is safe. The ceiling is set by information density (names, dates), not words per minute alone. This is inferred from the audio-visual protection finding.
- These studies measure comprehension of lectures by motivated students. Feed viewers who can swipe may tolerate less density. Untested.

### Gaps
- No study of narration rate vs retention specifically in Shorts or TikTok was found.
- I could not verify Guo et al.'s exact wpm bins from the primary PDF in this session.

## 6. On-screen captions and subtitles

### Takeaway
The much-quoted Verizon Media/Publicis (2019) survey shows *stated* preferences: 69% watch with sound off in public, and 80% say they are more likely to finish a video that has captions. The common "80% more likely to finish" phrasing misreads the survey. Academic evidence on learning is mixed. Captions help comprehension for some learners and for second-language learners, but the redundancy effect can hurt deeper learning. The one large Douyin science-video study found subtitles had a stable positive effect on how widely videos spread, while another Douyin study (6,606 videos) found subtitles negatively associated with comments and shares. No 2023-2026 Shorts-specific retention test of captions vs no captions was found.

### Cited Findings
- **(a, survey) Verizon Media and Publicis Media (April 2019), 5,616 US adults aged 18-54:**
  - 69% watch video with sound off in public places.
  - 80% say they are more likely to watch an entire video when captions are available.
  - Captions were linked to about an 8% lift in ad recall.

  Mobile sound-off figures of "92%" or "95%" circulate inconsistently. The original report was not reachable. Grade: moderate (large survey; self-report, not behaviour; 2019). [3Play Media](https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/verizon-media-and-publicis-media-find-viewers-want-captions/); [Streaming Media](https://www.streamingmedia.com/Articles/News/Online-Video-News/80-of-Video-Caption-Users-Arent-Hearing-Impaired-Finds-Verizon-131860.aspx); [NextTV](https://www.nexttv.com/news/mobile-videos-often-watched-without-audio-study-finds)
- **Misquote warning:** Kapwing and others restate the survey as "80% more likely to watch an entire video", which is a misreading. [Kapwing](https://kapwing.com/resources/subtitle-statistics)
- **(b) Douyin science and technology journal videos (4,422 videos):** subtitles and clear covers have "stable effects" on how widely videos spread. Grade: moderate. [PMC12854476](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12854476/)
- **(b) Douyin study of 6,606 videos (negative binomial regression):** subtitles negatively affected comments and shares, while vocal background music had a positive effect on likes, comments and shares. This contradicts the study above. Grade: moderate. Seen in search summary as a related study; the exact citation could not be pinned down, possibly [International Journal of Advertising, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/02650487.2026.2670858)
- **(b) Learning research:**
  - Subtitles helped comprehension in one study.
  - Another found subtitled video *worse* for deep learning outcomes, consistent with the redundancy principle (same words on screen and in narration).
  - Meta-analysis: captioned or subtitled video improves second-language vocabulary acquisition.

  Grade: moderate, mixed. [Tarchi et al. 2021 (Florence)](https://flore.unifi.it/bitstream/2158/1206797/2/Tarchi%202021%20Learning%20from%20media.pdf); [Hogrefe, J. Media Psychology](https://econtent.hogrefe.com/doi/10.1027/1864-1105/a000208); [Cambridge, Language Teaching](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/language-teaching/article/second-or-foreign-language-learning-through-watching-audiovisual-input-and-the-role-of-onscreen-text/7CBDDEC3187825A0776C521C6842A75B)
- **(b, small) Sheynin (TikTok):** having a text overlay was associated with higher engagement. Grade: weak. [PDF](http://www.theyoungresearcher.com/papers/sheynin.pdf)

### Inferences
- For feed viewing, much of which is sound-off, captions likely protect against losing silent viewers. This is supported by survey data, not by measured retention.
- To limit redundancy costs, keep captions short (keywords, names, dates) rather than verbatim paragraphs. This is inferred from learning research, not tested on Shorts.

### Gaps
- No behavioural, platform-measured test of captions vs no captions on Shorts retention (2023-2026) was found.
- No current (2023+) sound-off share for YouTube Shorts specifically was found. Shorts have audio on by default, which may differ from the 2019 survey context.

## 7. Looping and rewatch

### Takeaway
Since 31 March 2025, every play or replay of a Short counts as a public "view", so seamless loops mechanically inflate view counts. The older metric, renamed "engaged views", is what drives monetisation, and per a secondary source it excludes loops. **No data study was found showing that seamless loops raise recommendation reach, engaged views or retention.** "Loop it for the algorithm" remains lore.

### Cited Findings
- **YouTube change, effective 31 March 2025:** a view counts any time a Short starts to play or replay, with no minimum watch time. The old metric becomes "engaged views" in Advanced Mode analytics, and Partner Program eligibility and revenue still use engaged views. Grade: strong (platform announcement, widely reported). [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/03/26/youtube-shorts-views-counting-stats/); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/03/26/youtube-shorts-views-metric/)
- **Replays count as new views,** so a seamless loop earns a view on every pass. Grade: strong. [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2025/03/27/how-does-youtube-shorts-count-views-2025/)
- **Engaged views are said to exclude loops,** so one viewer making one pass is one engaged view. This comes from a secondary source quoting YouTube help and was not verified against YouTube's page. Grade: moderate-weak. [Mubert blog](https://mubert.com/blog/?p=5501)
- **(b) Kuaishou research** notes that "noisy watching" (including replays) contaminates watch-time signals, and platforms try to de-noise it. This suggests replays are not naively rewarded by ranking. Grade: moderate (other platform). [arXiv 2308.08120](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.08120v1)

### Inferences
- Loops can push APV above 100% and raise public view counts. Whether YouTube's ranker rewards loop-driven rewatches is not public. Use loops for their aesthetic and narrative payoff, not as a proven ranking lever.

### Gaps
- No measured study of seamless vs non-seamless endings and their effect on reach or engaged views was found.

## 8. Features of educational short videos that correlate with engagement (academic, 2020-2026)

### Takeaway
Cross-sectional studies of Douyin, TikTok and YouTube educational or health videos consistently find the following:
- Moderate duration (about 30-60 s) and higher visual quality go with wider spread.
- Pure entertainment with low information underperforms on deeper engagement.
- Measured content quality correlates only weakly with likes.
- Engagement metrics favour short, fast clips, while learning outcomes favour fuller explanations.

All of this is correlational and niche-specific.

### Cited Findings
- **(b) Douyin science journals, 4,422 videos:**
  - Duration and visual quality correlate positively with how widely videos spread.
  - Subtitles and clear covers have stable effects.
  - 30-60 s is optimal for balancing information density and cognitive load.

  Grade: moderate. [PMC12854476](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12854476/)
- **(b) Douyin sex-education videos ("Functional Infotainment"):** low-information, high-entertainment videos performed worst on deeper engagement and discussion. Substance plus entertainment won. Grade: moderate. [PMC12466664](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12466664/)
- **(b) Health education videos on YouTube and Douyin:** apart from like volume on Douyin (r = 0.25-0.4, P < 0.001), engagement did not correlate significantly with video quality. Grade: moderate. [BMC Medical Education](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12909-025-08018-5)
- **(b) TikTok/Douyin age-related macular degeneration videos:** duration is mildly positive with quality and engagement (r = 0.23-0.30), and quality is moderately positive with engagement. Grade: moderate-weak. [PMC12832813](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12832813/)
- **(b) Douyin vs Bilibili sports-health videos, 400 videos:** Douyin produces far higher immediate reactions (likes, comments, shares), while favourites converge across platforms. Grade: moderate-weak. [PMC13128596](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13128596/)
- **(b) Douyin study, 6,606 videos:**
  - Informativeness, emotional intensity and interactivity affect likes, comments and shares differently.
  - Vocal background music is positive for all three.
  - Subtitles are negative for comments and shares.

  Grade: moderate (citation not pinned down; see section 6).
- **(b) Manasrah et al. (2021), online learning:** 6-10 minute "short" videos improved viewing time by 24.7% and exam scores by 9.0% compared with longer videos. These are not short-form, but the result supports chunking. Grade: moderate. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/353491805_Short_Videos_or_Long_Videos_A_Study_on_the_Ideal_Video_Length_in_Online_Learning)
- **(b) Bibliometric review of educational short videos (arXiv, 2026):** maps the field and its operationalisation, and notes heterogeneous measures. Grade: n/a (review). [arXiv 2608.06932](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.06932)
- **(c/unverified) "A 2025 study found cutting educational content from 10 min to 90 s improved recall by 22% but reduced retention of complex concepts by 41%".** The original could not be located. Do not use. Grade: unverified.

### Inferences
- For history Shorts, the evidence favours:
  - genuinely informative content with an emotional or story frame over pure spectacle
  - high visual polish
  - a 30-60 s window

  These are correlations, not causal levers.
- Likes don't track accuracy or quality much, so quality is a brand choice rather than an algorithmic one.

### Gaps
- No academic study specific to *history* short videos, or to *animated* educational shorts, was found.
- No academic study using YouTube Shorts retention data (as opposed to likes and shares) was found. Researchers can't access YouTube retention metrics for other people's videos.
