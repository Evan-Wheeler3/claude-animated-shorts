# "The Tower They Saved by Digging" (Leaning Tower of Pisa) — script v1 for approval (2026-11-13)
Format: ~45 s vertical short, one steady narrator.

Facts (checked; sources disagree on some numbers, noted):
- Construction began August 1173; the tower leans because it stands on soft ground (Ingenia).
- Closed to the public in 1990 over fears its foundations could fail; handed back June 2001, reopened December 2001.
- 1993: 600 tonnes of lead on the north (high) side; raised to 900 tonnes in 1995 (Ingenia; other totals 870-1,000 t).
- Freezing the ground with liquid nitrogen (to anchor cables) caused trouble: frozen groundwater expanded, then the
  tower settled into the gaps; the plan was halted (NOVA, Gulf News). Exact date disputed (1990 vs 1995).
- The fix: controlled removal of small volumes of soil from beneath the north side (Burland; Soils and Foundations),
  about 38 m3 / 70 t in total; the tower came back about 50 cm (Gulf News; a Trevi page gives ~17 cm). The lean was
  deliberately left intact.
Sources: ingenia.org.uk (stabilisation of the Leaning Tower of Pisa) · pbs.org/wgbh/nova/pisa · gulfnews.com (A structural revival) ·
jstage.jst.go.jp (Soils and Foundations 43/5) · trevispa.com

In 1990, Italy closed the Leaning Tower of Pisa, because it was about to fall over.
It started leaning while it was still being built, eight hundred years ago. The ground under it is soft clay.
By 1990, it leaned so far that engineers feared it could collapse any day.
First, they stacked hundreds of tons of lead on the high side, just to hold it still.
Then they tried freezing the ground solid, and the tower started sinking even faster.
So they did the opposite of building. They dug.
Very carefully, they pulled soil out from under the high side, a little at a time.
And slowly, the tower settled back, about half a metre at the top.
Then they stopped. On purpose.
Because nobody flies across the world to pretend they're holding up a straight tower.
