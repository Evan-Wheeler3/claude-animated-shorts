# "The Great Emu War" — original script v1 (2026-11-12)
Format: ~66 s vertical short, ~3.7 words/s, one steady narrator, no captions.

Facts (checked; see sources):
- Western Australia, Campion district, 2 Nov - 10 Dec 1932. About 20,000 emus (an estimate) moved in from the interior
  after breeding and ate and trampled the wheat. The farmers, many of them WWI veterans settled on the land, asked for
  machine guns.
- The army sent Major G.P.W. Meredith (Royal Australian Artillery), two soldiers, two Lewis guns and 10,000 rounds.
- First contact near Campion: about 50 emus, out of range; the birds scattered.
- 4 Nov: an ambush at a dam; more than 1,000 emus approached; the gunners held fire to point-blank range, and the gun
  jammed after about a dozen birds. The rest ran.
- Meredith mounted a gun on a truck; the truck could not gain on the birds (Wikipedia, "Emu War": the ride was also too
  rough to shoot). The search confirmed the mounting; the "could not catch them" detail is from the Wikipedia article.
- Meredith said emus "can face machine guns with the invulnerability of tanks". Ornithologist Dominic Serventy wrote
  that the emus adopted guerrilla tactics and split into small units.
- No human casualties. Meredith claimed 986 birds for 9,860 rounds: exactly 10 rounds a bird. The army withdrew.
- 1934: a bounty scheme; 57,034 bounties claimed in six months. Emus run at about 50 km/h (30 mph).

In 1932, Australia went to war with twenty thousand birds, and lost.
Out west, about twenty thousand emus walk in from the desert and start eating the wheat.
The farmers are veterans of the Great War, so they ask for the one thing they trust. Machine guns.
The army sends a major, two soldiers, two machine guns, and ten thousand bullets.
On day one, they spot fifty emus. The birds simply stroll out of range.
So the major sets an ambush at a watering hole, and a thousand emus walk right into it.
The gunners wait until point-blank range, open fire, and after about twelve birds, the gun jams.
Next plan. Bolt a gun onto a truck. The emus outrun the truck.
The major reports that emus can face machine guns with the invulnerability of tanks.
Luckily, the army suffered zero casualties.
Unluckily, so did almost all of the emus.
You see, a machine gun is built for an enemy that charges at you in a crowd.
Ever chased pigeons in a park? The second you move, they scatter in fifty directions.
The emus split into small groups, and ran at thirty miles an hour.
After a month, the army went home, having fired ten bullets for every bird.
Two years later, the government just paid the farmers per emu, and they collected on fifty-seven thousand.
So the war was finally won by the same farmers who had called in the army.
