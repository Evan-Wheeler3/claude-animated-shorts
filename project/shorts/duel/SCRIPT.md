# "Could You Survive a Western Duel?" — script v2 (the user's trimmed version, 2026-11-13)
Format: ~45 s vertical short, second person, one steady narrator. One on-screen element: an odds-of-survival counter
on a wooden sign at the top of frame (the user's choice; the numbers are a game, not measured odds). Bloodless.

Facts behind the lines (checked):
- Classic face-to-face quick-draw duels were rare; the best-known real one, Wild Bill Hickok vs Davis Tutt
  (Springfield, Missouri, 21 Jul 1865), was fought about 75 yards apart (Wikipedia, History.com, Legends of America).
- The Colt Single Action Army (introduced 1873) must be cocked by hand before every shot.
- Gunfighters missed a lot: the O.K. Corral fight (1881) saw about 30 shots in about 30 seconds, some fighters about
  6 ft apart, with roughly 10-16 hits depending on the account (Wikipedia, True West, Arizona Highways).
- Reacting second makes the hand move faster (about 21 ms), but noticing takes about 200 ms, so the reactor rarely
  wins (Welchman et al. 2010, Proc. R. Soc. B; National Geographic). This is why "you won't be fast enough".

You wake up in 1873. A saloon in Kansas.
You just spilled whiskey all over a notorious gunfighter.
He doesn't want an apology.
He wants you outside.
Good news: Real Wild West duels were surprisingly rare.
Bad news: You've never shot a single action revolver at a target seventy-five yards away.
And the guy across from you? He's been shooting his entire life.
But here's your advantage.
Real gunfighters missed. A LOT.
So what if you let him draw first, while you take time to aim?
Not a bad idea, but this only works if he misses his first shot. If not... well, you simply won't be fast enough.
But there IS one strategy with much better odds of keeping you alive.
Walk back into the saloon.
And buy the man a new drink.
