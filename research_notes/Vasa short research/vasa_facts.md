## 1. Commissioning and build (contract, keel, launch, builders, the King, Fleming)

### Takeaway
The contract was signed in January 1625, building started in early 1626, and the ship was launched in spring 1627. Henrik Hybertsson designed it. He fell ill and died in 1627, and his assistant Hein Jacobsson finished it. The museum and Hocker-derived sources say the King approved the dimensions. The popular claim that he forced a second gun deck in mid-build is not supported: the King ordered enough guns for two decks within months of the keel being laid.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED.** In January 1625 the King signed a contract with shipbuilder Henrik Hybertsson and his business partner Arendt de Groote for four new ships. Vasa and Äpplet were the two large ones; the two smaller ships were never built. — [Vasa Museum: Timeline](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/timeline); [Vasa Museum FAQ: Who built the ship?](https://faq.vasamuseet.se/category/45/article/1259?lang=en)
- **DISPUTED (minor).** One museum page dates the Hybertsson partnership's navy-yard contract from 1626, which conflicts with the January 1625 signing. — [Vasa Museum: The Ship Yard](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/ship-yard)
- **CONFIRMED, approximate date.** The keel was laid in late winter 1626; a museum forum answer says February or March 1626. The museum's facts page gives building as 1626 to 1628. No exact keel-laying day was found. — [Timeline](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/timeline); [Vasa Museum forum (Swedish): Vem byggde skeppet Vasa?](https://forum.vasamuseet.se/org/vasamuseet/d/vem-byggde-skeppet-vasa/); [Facts about Vasa](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/facts-about-vasa)
- **CONFIRMED.** The ship was launched in spring 1627. The hull was largely finished by then; rigging, fitting out and arming took about another year, and late cannon casting delayed completion. — [Timeline](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/timeline); [Vasa genom åren (Swedish timeline)](https://www.vasamuseet.se/utforska/vasas-historia/tidslinje)
- **CONFIRMED / DISPUTED date.** Hybertsson fell ill and handed day-to-day work to his assistant Hein Jacobsson (also spelled Jakobsson) during 1626. He died in spring or May 1627 according to the forum answer, or "about a year before the 1628 inquest" according to the timeline; these are consistent. His widow Margareta Nilsdotter is said to have taken part in running the business afterwards. — [Timeline](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/timeline); [Vasa Museum forum](https://forum.vasamuseet.se/org/vasamuseet/d/vem-byggde-skeppet-vasa/); [The Ship Yard](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/ship-yard)
- **CONFIRMED.** The ship was built at the navy yard (Skeppsgården) on what was then called Skeppsholmen, now the Blasieholmen peninsula. The Strand Hotel stands on the site today. — [Vasa Museum: The Ship Yard](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/ship-yard); [Skeppsgården (Swedish)](https://www.vasamuseet.se/utforska/vasas-historia/skeppsgarden)
- **King's role, CONFIRMED (museum).** At the inquiry, Hein Jacobsson and Arendt de Groote said the ship was built to the design and dimensions the King himself had approved. De Groote said he had shown the King a picture of a similar ship as a model. — [Vasa Museum: The Inquest](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/inquest); [Audioguide 8: The Inquiry](https://www.vasamuseet.se/besok/audioguide/audioguide-english/textversion/8.-the-inquiry)
- **Second gun deck, DISPUTED.** The secondary evidence, which derives from Hocker's work, points against the myth:
  - Myth version: the King ordered a second gun deck after hearing that the Danes were building a two-decker. This appears in popular retellings, which cite no documents. — [JW.org (popular)](https://www.jw.org/en/library/magazines/g20000408/The-Vasa-From-Disaster-to-Attraction/); [Beton Consulting (popular)](https://www.betonconsultingeng.com/what-can-we-learn-from-the-vasa/)
  - Counter-evidence: on 5 August 1626 the King ordered seventy-two 24-pounder guns for the ship. That is too many for one gun deck, and it came less than five months after building started, early enough for two decks to be in the design from the start. There is "little evidence" that Vasa was substantially modified after the keel was laid. This comes from a mirror of an older Wikipedia article that cites Hocker's *Vasa I*; I could not read Hocker directly. — [en-academic mirror of Wikipedia "Vasa (ship)"](https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/125020)
- **King's deadline, UNVERIFIED (secondary).** The King had ordered the ship ready by 25 July 1628, so the 10 August sailing was already more than two weeks late. — [University of Portland Vasa case study (PDF)](http://faculty.up.edu/lulay/failure/Vasacasestudy.pdf)
- **CONFIRMED.** Hein Jacobsson admitted he had widened the ship slightly: about 40 cm in the English museum text, about 42 cm in the Swedish text. — [The Inquest](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/inquest); [Utredningen (Swedish)](https://www.vasamuseet.se/utforska/vasas-historia/utredningen)

### Inferences
- A safe line for the script: "The King approved the design and wanted it fast." Avoid "the King forced a second deck."
- The King was abroad (campaigning in Prussia) in August 1628. This is implied by Fleming's reported "if only His Majesty were at home" remark (section 3), but I did not find it stated directly; see Gaps.

### Gaps
- Fleming's exact title in 1628 (Admiral or Vice-Admiral) and his formal role were not confirmed. One secondary source calls him Vice-Admiral ([Discover Magazine](https://www.discovermagazine.com/the-sciences/the-vasa-warship-sank-after-only-25-minutes-of-sailing-taking-30-lives-with)).
- Where the King was in August 1628 was not directly sourced.
- I had no access to Hocker's own text on the second-deck question.

## 2. Ship specs

### Takeaway
Use the Vasa Museum's figures: 64 guns (48 of them 24-pounders), 120 tonnes of stone ballast, about 52 m from keel to mainmast top, and 98% original. Length depends on how it is measured (69 m vs 60.93 m), so give the method with the number.

### Cited Findings
- **Length, DISPUTED (it depends on the measurement).**
  - 69 m long when completed. This is the museum timeline figure and probably includes the bowsprit. — [Timeline](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/timeline)
  - 60.93 m measured overall, nose of figurehead to taffrail. — [Vasa Museum FAQ: precise dimensions](https://faq.vasamuseet.se/category/45/article/4707?lang=en)
  - 38.32 m length on the keel. — [Vasa Museum FAQ: precise dimensions](https://faq.vasamuseet.se/category/45/article/4707?lang=en)
- **Height, CONFIRMED as an estimate.**
  - 52.4 m from the bottom of the keel to the top of the mainmast. The museum's FAQ says this is an estimate, because the upper mast sections did not survive. — [FAQ: precise dimensions](https://faq.vasamuseet.se/category/45/article/4707?lang=en)
  - Wikipedia says 52.5 m; the timeline says "more than 50 m". — [Timeline](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/timeline); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasa_(ship))
- **Quarterdeck height, UNVERIFIED (blog).** About 19.5 m above the water. — [Lost Fort blog](https://lostfort.blogspot.com/2015/08/the-ship-that-never-sailed-vasa-museum.html)
- **Guns, CONFIRMED.**
  - 64 cannons aboard on the maiden voyage, with eight gunports empty. The main armament was 48 24-pounders. — [Vasa Museum: Machine of war](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/machine-of-war); [FAQ: The Ship & its History](https://faq.vasamuseet.se/category/45?lang=en)
  - There were two gun decks.
- **Ballast, CONFIRMED.** 120 tonnes of stone ballast. — [FAQ: The Ship & its History](https://faq.vasamuseet.se/category/45?lang=en)
- **Sails, UNVERIFIED (Wikipedia-derived; no museum page found).**
  - Ten sails, about 1,275 m² of sail area, about 1,210 tonnes displacement. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasa_(ship)); [yacht.de](https://www.yacht.de/en/yachts/classics/vasa-the-biggest-misconstruction-of-modern-times/)
  - The museum does confirm "ten sails", indirectly: four of the ten were set on departure (section 4).
- **Sculptures, CONFIRMED but vague.** The museum describes "hundreds of sculptures". Its scale model carries "more than 500 sculptures". — [Machine of war](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/machine-of-war); [The Vasa Model](https://vasamuseet.se/en/exhibitions/vasa-model)
- **Originality, CONFIRMED.** The museum says Vasa is 98% original. The museum's head of research recalculated it, finding about 2% modern replacement, such as new deck planks. The older figure in circulation was 95%. — [Vasa Museum forum: How much of Vasa is original?](https://forum.vasamuseet.se/org/vasamuseet-en/d/how-original-is-the-vasa/)
- **Crew planned, CONFIRMED.** The navy's 1628 plan was 133 sailors and 300 soldiers. The soldiers were not aboard when the ship sank. — [Vasa Museum: Life on board](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/life-on-board)

### Inferences
- For a cartoon: "about 69 m long and over 50 m tall, roughly a 17-storey building." The building comparison is my own approximation.
- The commonly quoted "over 700 sculptures and ornaments" did not appear in what I found. Use "hundreds" or "more than 500" (the model figure).

### Gaps
- No museum figure was found for the beam. It is commonly quoted as 11.7 m, but that is **UNVERIFIED** here.
- No museum figure was found for sail area or displacement.

## 3. The stability (heeling) test before departure

### Takeaway
About 30 men ran back and forth across the deck while the ship was moored. It heeled so badly that the test was stopped for fear it would capsize at the quay. Admiral Klas Fleming was present, but nothing was done. The "three runs" detail and the exact wording of Fleming's remark come only from secondary sources.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED.** At the quay, about thirty men ran across the deck, and "the ship was heeling so badly that there was great fear it might capsize at the quay." — [Vasa Museum: The Disaster](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster)
- **CONFIRMED (secondary, several sources agree).** The test was stopped because of the heeling. — [Britannia Naval Research Association](https://www.britannia-naval-research-association.com/articles/the-vasa-story/); [CMU course page "The Vasa Capsizes" (adapted from Kvarning / Scientific American)](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/67-325/vasa.html)
- **UNVERIFIED.** The test was halted after three runs across the deck (a "lurch" test). — [University of Portland case study (PDF)](http://faculty.up.edu/lulay/failure/Vasacasestudy.pdf); [CMU course page](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/67-325/vasa.html)
- **DISPUTED.** Who initiated the test:
  - Captain Söfring Hansson demonstrated the problem to Fleming. — [Discover Magazine](https://www.discovermagazine.com/the-sciences/the-vasa-warship-sank-after-only-25-minutes-of-sailing-taking-30-lives-with)
  - "Admiral Fleming had arranged a stability test." — [CMU course page](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/67-325/vasa.html)
- **UNVERIFIED wording.** Fleming's remark is reported as "If only His Majesty were at home" or "If only the King were here." This is secondhand, and the phrasing varies between sources. — [University of Portland case study](http://faculty.up.edu/lulay/failure/Vasacasestudy.pdf); [Psych Safety](https://psychsafety.com/the-vasa-disaster/)
- **UNVERIFIED.** The shipbuilders were not present at the test and were never told the results. Whether Fleming and Hansson deliberately withheld them is speculation. — [University of Portland case study](http://faculty.up.edu/lulay/failure/Vasacasestudy.pdf)

### Inferences
- A good beat for the short: men running side to side, the ship rolling, an officer waving them to stop. The "30 men" figure is safe; "three runs" should be treated as an optional detail.
- Do not put Fleming's remark on screen as a direct quote unless someone checks it against the museum's inquiry transcript ([original documents](https://www.vasamuseet.se/skola/i-klassrummet/originaldokument-om-vasa)).

### Gaps
- I could not read the original 1628 testimony. The museum publishes the documents (Swedish) at the original-documents link above.
- Date of the test: not found.

## 4. The sinking

### Takeaway
On Sunday afternoon, 10 August 1628, Vasa cast off below Tre Kronor castle between 4 and 5 pm. It set four of its ten sails and fired a salute. It drifted in light wind under the Södermalm cliffs. A gust at Tegelviken heeled it to port, water poured in through the open lower gunports, and it sank to 32 m. It had sailed about 1,300 m in about 20 minutes. About 150 people were aboard, and at least 30 died.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED.**
  - Sunday afternoon, 10 August 1628: Vasa casts off from below the Tre Kronor castle. — [Vasa genom åren (Swedish)](https://www.vasamuseet.se/utforska/vasas-historia/tidslinje)
  - It cast off "between four and five o'clock". — [The Disaster](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster)
- **CONFIRMED.** Four of the ten sails were set and a salute was fired. — [The Disaster](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster)
- **CONFIRMED (weather).**
  - There was little wind under the bluffs of Södermalm, "not even enough to pull the sheets of the sails taut". Vasa drifted on the current.
  - At the gap in the bluffs at Tegelviken, "a much stronger gust" heeled the ship to port, and water poured in through the open gunports on the lower gun deck.
  - Source: [The Disaster](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster)
- **CONFIRMED.**
  - Within minutes the ship lay on the seabed at 32 m.
  - Some survivors swam the 120 m to the shore of Beckholmen.
  - Sources: [The Disaster](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster); [Timeline](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/timeline)
- **CONFIRMED (museum), but treat it as approximate.** Distance sailed:
  - The museum gives about 1,300 m. — [The Disaster](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster)
  - Others give "barely 1,300 m" or "1,300 m (1,400 yd)". — [Mary Rose Trust](https://maryrose.org/the-tale-of-two-ships-vasa-lost-on-her-maiden-voyage/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasa_(ship))
  - One popular source says "less than a mile". — [HistoryDaily](https://www.historydaily.com/the-sinking-of-the-vasa/)
  - 1,300 m is about 0.7 nautical miles (my conversion).
  - **Caution:** "120 m" is the distance **from shore** (Beckholmen), not the distance sailed. Some sources blur the two. — [The Disaster](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster)
- **DISPUTED (duration).**
  - Museum: "Vasa sailed for only about 20 minutes" before taking in water. — [Vasa Museum search result, The Disaster / Facts](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster)
  - Discover Magazine says "25 minutes". — [Discover Magazine](https://www.discovermagazine.com/the-vasa-warship-sank-after-only-25-minutes-of-sailing-taking-30-lives-with-47131)
  - Another source says "less than an hour".
- **CONFIRMED.** About 150 people were aboard, and at least 30 died ("Av de ungefär 150 människorna ombord dog minst 30"). — [Vasa genom åren (Swedish)](https://www.vasamuseet.se/utforska/vasas-historia/tidslinje)
- **DISPUTED (deaths).**
  - The museum says "roughly thirty". — [Vasa Museum: Skeletons](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/research/skeletons)
  - Contemporary letters give about 50, 40 and 30. — [Vasa Museum FAQ: How many people died?](https://faq.vasamuseet.se/category/45/article/1282?lang=en)
  - Probably a few more died than the skeletons show, because some bodies were recovered and buried at the time. — [FAQ](https://faq.vasamuseet.se/category/45/article/1282?lang=en)
- **CONFIRMED.** Several letters show that women and children were aboard. Some may have been guests; others may have been crew, such as servants or cabin boys. — [Vasa Museum (search excerpt, Disaster / Life on board)](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster)
- **CONFIRMED.** Most of the dead were trapped inside the ship. — [The Disaster](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster)
- **CONFIRMED.** Leaving the gunports open was decisive. If they had been closed, the museum says the ship might have survived and been rebuilt into a more stable configuration. — [The Disaster](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster)
- **CONFIRMED (cause).** The upper works were too heavy and tall for the hull below the waterline, giving a high centre of gravity. Even a light breeze could heel it. — [The Disaster](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster)

### Inferences
- The museum's own figures for distance (about 1,300 m) and time (about 20 minutes) are usable. Phrase them as "about" and "barely a kilometre and a half".

### Gaps
- Crowds and foreign diplomats watching: I found no source in this session. It is widely repeated, but **UNVERIFIED** here. A letter from a foreign diplomat in Stockholm is said to exist; not confirmed.
- No exact time of the capsize was found beyond "cast off 4 to 5 pm, about 20 minutes later".
- Wind direction was not found.

## 5. The inquiry

### Takeaway
The Council of State (Riksrådet) opened an inquiry the day after the sinking. The builders said the King had approved the dimensions. Captain Söfring Hansson was questioned. Nobody was punished, and the blame effectively fell on the dead Henrik Hybertsson.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED.**
  - The inquiry began the day after the sinking, held before the Council of State and the Admiralty.
  - Nobody was ever punished, and the designer Hybertsson, who had died in 1627, effectively took the blame.
  - Sources: [The Inquest](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/inquest); [The Disaster](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster)
- **CONFIRMED.** Questioned:
  - Captain Söfring Hansson, who went on to a successful career in the navy.
  - Hein Jacobsson, the builder.
  - Arendt de Groote, the contractor and partner (one museum text names "Arendt Hybertsson" instead, an inconsistency).
  - Hein would not move from his defence that he built to the King-approved design, despite increasingly insistent questioning by the prosecutor.
  - Sources: [The Inquest](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/inquest); [Audioguide 8: The Inquiry](https://www.vasamuseet.se/besok/audioguide/audioguide-english/textversion/8.-the-inquiry); [Förhören (Swedish)](https://www.vasamuseet.se/besok/audioguide/audioguide-svenska/textversion/8.-forhoren)
- **CONFIRMED (museum search excerpt).** No one was officially blamed. Those questioned were eventually promoted. Hein worked at the navy yard until he retired in 1638. — [The Inquest](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/inquest)
- The museum publishes the original 1628 documents on the sinking (Swedish). — [Ta del av dokumenten från 1628](https://www.vasamuseet.se/skola/i-klassrummet/originaldokument-om-vasa)

### Inferences
- Script line: "Who was to blame? Officially: no one."

### Gaps
- Whether Fleming testified, and what he said, is not confirmed in what I found.

## 6. 1660s salvage of the bronze cannons

### Takeaway
In the 1660s, Albrecht von Treileben and Andreas Peckell used a diving bell at about 30 m and raised most of the bronze guns. The museum gives 61 of the 64 in one place and 55 in another.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED.** Treileben received the salvage right in 1663. He and his German colleague Andreas Peckell used a diving bell, working at about 30 m in darkness and cold. They removed the guns from their carriages, damaging much of the upper deck. The guns were sold abroad. — [Audioguide 9: The First Salvage Efforts](https://www.vasamuseet.se/besok/audioguide/audioguide-english/textversion/9.-the-first-salvage-efforts); [Timeline](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/timeline); [Vasa genom åren](https://www.vasamuseet.se/utforska/vasas-historia/tidslinje)
- **DISPUTED (count).** Both figures are from the museum:
  - 61 cannons salvaged in the 17th century. — [Battle! exhibition](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/visit/exhibitions/battle); [Vasa genom åren](https://www.vasamuseet.se/utforska/vasas-historia/tidslinje)
  - 55 cannons. — [Audioguide 9](https://www.vasamuseet.se/besok/audioguide/audioguide-english/textversion/9.-the-first-salvage-efforts)
- **CONFIRMED.** An Italian priest visiting Stockholm watched the divers in 1663 and described it in his diary. — [Audioguide 9](https://www.vasamuseet.se/besok/audioguide/audioguide-english/textversion/9.-the-first-salvage-efforts)
- Name spelling varies across museum pages: Albrekt or Albrecht von Treileben.

### Inferences
- Safe phrasing: "most of the guns" or "over 50 bronze cannons".

### Gaps
- The exact years of the main lifts (1664 to 1665 is commonly cited) were not confirmed in this session.

## 7. Rediscovery, raising, preservation, PEG

### Takeaway
Anders Franzén found the wreck off Beckholmen in 1956. It broke the surface at 09:03 on 24 April 1961. It survived because the Baltic's low salinity keeps out shipworm. It was sprayed with PEG from 1962 to 1979, then slowly air-dried for about nine years.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED.**
  - Anders Franzén, a fuels engineer, struck wood with his corer on 25 August 1956.
  - Divers confirmed on 4 September 1956 that it was a ship.
  - The location was off Beckholmen. Archives pointed to the north side of the channel, whereas a 1920s historian had placed it on the south side.
  - Sources: [Timeline](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/timeline); [The Search for Vasa](https://vasamuseet.se/en/vasa-history/search-for-vasa); [The Salvage](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/salvage)
- **CONFIRMED.** Vasa broke the surface at 9:03 am on 24 April 1961, in front of international TV cameras, after 333 years on the seabed. — [Audioguide 10: The Successful Salvage](https://www.vasamuseet.se/besok/audioguide/audioguide-english/textversion/10.-the-successfull-salvage); [The Salvage](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/salvage)
- **CONFIRMED.** Franzén reasoned that because of low salinity and the absence of shipworm in the Baltic, the oak could be well preserved. — [The Salvage](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/salvage)
- **CONFIRMED.**
  - Conservation with polyethylene glycol (PEG), which replaces the water in the wood cells so they do not shrink and crack.
  - Spraying ran from April 1962 to January 1979 (17 years), followed by about 9 years of slow air-drying.
  - Sources: [Vasa Museum: Conservation](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/research/conservation); [Timeline](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/timeline)
- **CONFIRMED.**
  - Skeletons of at least 17 people were found during the excavation, with more remains found on dives in 1966.
  - 11 were found on the decks and 4 in the stern-gallery wreckage.
  - Sources: [Vasa Museum: Skeletons](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/research/skeletons); [The Excavation](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/excavation)
- **CONFIRMED.** Over 4,000 coins were found, some of them in the pockets of clothes worn by skeletons. — [The Excavation](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/excavation)

### Inferences
- The museum uses "333 years", from 1628 to 1961.

### Gaps
- None critical.

## 8. Vasa Museum: opening, visitors, % original

### Takeaway
The museum opened on 15 June 1990. It is officially described as "the most visited museum in Scandinavia", with about 1.5 million visitors a year. Recent years are lower: 1.35 million in 2024. Vasa is 98% original.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED.** The ship was moved in 1988 and the museum opened in 1990; Wikidata gives the opening date as 15 June 1990. — [Wikidata Q901371](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q901371); [Wikipedia: Vasa Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasa_Museum)
- **CONFIRMED (museum's own claim).** "The Vasa Museum in Stockholm, with 1.5 million visitors per year, is the most visited museum in Scandinavia." This page dates from the COVID-era financial notice, so it describes pre-pandemic levels. — [Vasa Museum: Information about the financial situation](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/about-the-vasa-museum/news/information-about-the-vasa-museums-financial-situation)

## 9. Visual details for design (paint, flags, clothing, harbour, Tre Kronor, weather)

### Takeaway
- The ship was brightly painted. The upper hull was red, and the sculptures were gilded or painted in red, blue and green.
- Sailors wore their own clothes; there was no uniform.
- The ship departed from below the Tre Kronor castle, which had a tall tower crowned with three crowns.
- The day had little wind, then gusts.
- No source documents the specific flags flown in 1628.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED (paint).**
  - Upper hull areas were red: bulwarks, transom, quarter galleries and the sides of the beakhead.
  - Sculptures were brightly painted, most likely in gold, red, blue and green. Hair, costumes, jewellery and weapons were gilded; yellow ochre was sometimes a cheaper substitute for gold leaf.
  - Pigments found include cinnabar (red) and indigo.
  - The research was led by conservator Peter Tångeberg (1990 to 2002), using over 1,000 paint samples and about 1,200 microscopic fragments. About 15 key sculptures have been reconstructed in colour.
  - An older theory, now superseded, had gilt or yellow figures on a blue background.
  - Sources: [Audioguide 7: Vasa Shows Its Colours](https://www.vasamuseet.se/besok/audioguide/audioguide-english/textversion/7.-vasa-shows-its-colours); [Vasa Museum: Art](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/art); [Colour the sculptures](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/visit/kids--families/colour-the-sculptures)
- **CONFIRMED (sailors' clothing).**
  - The navy issued no uniforms; men bought or made their own clothes.
  - Typical dress: linen shirt, short skirted woollen doublet, woollen knee-breeches ending below the knee, woollen socks and leather shoes.
  - Many wore broad-brimmed hats or conical caps.
  - Source: [Vasa Museum: Life on board](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/life-on-board)
- **CONFIRMED (departure point).** The ship cast off below the royal castle Tre Kronor and was warped toward Slussen before setting sail. — [Vasa genom åren](https://www.vasamuseet.se/utforska/vasas-historia/tidslinje); [The Disaster](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster)
- **UNVERIFIED (Tre Kronor's appearance).**
  - A medieval castle remodelled in Renaissance style under John III.
  - It had a tall central tower topped by three golden crowns, which gave the castle its name.
  - It burned on 7 May 1697, and the current palace stands on the site.
  - Sources: [Wikipedia: Tre Kronor (castle)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tre_Kronor_(castle)); [Stockholm Museum guide](https://www.stockholmmuseum.com/museums/castles/tre-kronor-castle.htm)
- **CONFIRMED (route landmarks).** In order: Tre Kronor quay, Slussen, the Södermalm bluffs, the gap at Tegelviken, then the capsize near Beckholmen. The navy yard was at Skeppsholmen/Blasieholmen. — [The Disaster](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster); [The Ship Yard](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/ship-yard)
- **CONFIRMED (weather).** Little wind under the cliffs, then sudden stronger gusts. Use "light, gusty breeze", not "storm". — [The Disaster](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster)
- **UNVERIFIED (flags).**
  - The ship was dressed with flags and fired a salute on departure, but no source names the exact ensign or pennants.
  - Swedish naval ensign regulations date only from 1663.
  - Sources: [hhogman.se: Ships of the Swedish Navy](https://www.hhogman.se/navy-ships-swe.htm); [Military History Now](https://militaryhistorynow.com/2017/11/29/a-fatal-first-voyage-how-europes-mightiest-warship-foundered-moments-into-her-inaugural-cruise/)

### Inferences
- A generic blue flag with a yellow cross is a reasonable stylised choice, but it is not documented for Vasa in 1628. Keep flags simple and do not claim accuracy for them.

## 10. Popular myths vs. solid facts

### Takeaway
Several "myths" in the brief are actually the museum's own figures: about 20 minutes and about 1,300 m. The weaker claims are that the King forced a second gun deck mid-build, exact death tolls, and dramatic quotes.

### Cited Findings
- **"Sank after 20 minutes": supported, as an approximation.** The museum says "about 20 minutes"; other sources say 25 minutes or "less than an hour". — [The Disaster](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster); [Discover Magazine](https://www.discovermagazine.com/the-vasa-warship-sank-after-only-25-minutes-of-sailing-taking-30-lives-with-47131)
- **"1,300 m": supported by the museum.** Do not confuse it with the 120 m to shore. — [The Disaster](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster)
- **"The King forced a second gun deck mid-construction": not supported.** The 1626 order for 72 guns shows two decks were planned early; there is "little evidence" of major post-keel changes (Hocker, via the mirror). What is supported is that the King approved the dimensions. — [en-academic Wikipedia mirror citing Hocker](https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/125020); [The Inquest](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/inquest)
- **"A Danish two-decker prompted the change": UNVERIFIED** popular claim. — [JW.org](https://www.jw.org/en/library/magazines/g20000408/The-Vasa-From-Disaster-to-Attraction/)
- **"Exact death toll of 30" or other precise numbers: DISPUTED.**
  - The museum says roughly 30 or at least 30.
  - Contemporary letters say 30 to 50.
  - At least 17 individuals were found as skeletons.
  - Sources: [FAQ](https://faq.vasamuseet.se/category/45/article/1282?lang=en); [Skeletons](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/research/skeletons)
- **"Nobody was punished": supported.** — [The Inquest](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/inquest)
- **"95% original": outdated.** The museum now says 98%. — [forum](https://forum.vasamuseet.se/org/vasamuseet-en/d/how-original-is-the-vasa/)
- **"Ship was painted gold on blue": outdated.** The paint research shows a red ground with polychrome and gilt sculptures. — [Art](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/art)
- **"Sank in a storm": wrong.** It was light wind and gusts. — [The Disaster](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster)

### Inferences
- Safest 30-45 s spine:
  1. King orders a huge two-deck warship (1625).
  2. The heeling test with about 30 men is stopped.
  3. 10 August 1628: salute fired, and about 1,300 m and about 20 minutes later a gust tips it.
  4. Water floods the open gunports; at least 30 die.
  5. Nobody is punished.
  6. Found in 1956, raised in 1961, 98% original; over a million visitors a year.
