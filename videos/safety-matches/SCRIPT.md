# Why Matches Only Light On The Box: script v2 (Fix-Fail Chain, clearer wording, for the script gate)

Narrator voice B. ~185 words (~63 s). Full sentences, one cause and effect per line; 'you' only where the viewer acts.
Title options: "Why Matches Only Light On The Box" · "Why Safety Matches Are Safe" · "The Match That Rotted Jaws"

L01 The oldest way to make fire is to rub two sticks together. But it takes so long that your arms give out.
L02 So people made chemical matches that burst into flame when you dipped them in acid. But then you had to carry a bottle of acid around.
L03 Next came matches that you dragged through sandpaper. But they stank of sulfur, and burning bits fell onto the carpet.
L04 So match makers switched to white phosphorus, which lit on anything you struck it against. But it lit so easily that it had to be kept in a sealed tin.
L05 Worse, it was poison. The fumes destroyed the jawbones of the factory workers, and their bones even glowed in the dark.
L06 So match makers split the match in two. The head got one set of chemicals, and the strip on the box got red phosphorus.
L07 Neither half can catch fire on its own. They only light when you rub them together. That is why it is called a safety match.
L08 So striking a match still works like the very first fire. You are rubbing two things together, with some chemical help!
