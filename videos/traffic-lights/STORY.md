# Why traffic lights are red, yellow and green: fact sheet (Wikipedia pages read 2026-11-26; quotes verbatim unless marked)

| # | Fact | Source | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | London 1866: "1,102 people were killed and 1,334 injured on roads in London" | [John Peake Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Peake_Knight) | Safe |
| F2 | First traffic light, 9 Dec 1868, near Westminster Bridge / outside the Houses of Parliament; railway-style semaphore arms by day, "red and green gas-powered lamps at night"; "operated by a policeman" ("A police constable raised or lowered the semaphore arms and, at night, operated a lever") | John Peake Knight, [Traffic light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_light) | Safe |
| F3 | "in 1869, a gas leak caused one of the lights to explode, badly injuring the policeman operating it" ("exploded on 2 January 1869 and was taken down") | John Peake Knight, Traffic light | Safe: "badly hurt" |
| F3x | The policeman was killed | Lester Wire article only, contradicted by the others | **Avoid** |
| F4 | "Traffic lights did not appear again in the United Kingdom until 1929" (1869 -> 1929 = 60 years) | John Peake Knight, William Potts | Safe: "Britain went sixty years without one" |
| F5 | Before signals, officers "would stand on small platforms in the middle of each intersection" directing traffic, "for long hours even in inclement weather"; traffic "very chaotic and dangerous" | [Lester Wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_Wire) | Safe |
| F6 | 1912, Lester Wire, "a policeman in Salt Lake City", first electric traffic light, red and green, "hand-operated by a police officer in a booth" | Traffic light, Lester Wire | Safe |
| F7 | Red and green "chosen for familiarity, as they were already being used for similar purposes as nautical lights and railway signals" | Lester Wire | Safe |
| F8 | "motorists unfamiliar with the traffic light simply chose to ignore it" | Lester Wire | Safe |
| F9 | Two-colour signals "did not leave drivers sufficient time to stop at high speeds"; "Some municipalities experimented with leaving the green on for a few seconds after the red was illuminated" | [William Potts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Potts_(inventor)) | Safe |
| F10 | William Potts, Detroit police, added an amber "caution" light, "to indicate the impending transition"; first four-way three-colour light, Detroit | Traffic light, William Potts | Safe without a year (1917 vs 1920 disputed) |
| F11 | Timers replaced traffic officers: "New York City was able to reassign all but 500 of its 6,000 officers working on the traffic squad" | Traffic light | Safe: "more than five thousand" |
| F12 | A buzzer warning on early lights | not in sources read | **Avoid** |
