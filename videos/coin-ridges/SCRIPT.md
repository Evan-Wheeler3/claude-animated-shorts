L01 People used to shave the edges off their coins, and then spend them anyway!
L02 Back then, a silver coin was worth the silver inside it.
L03 So shave a little off a hundred coins, melt the shavings, and you have free silver!
L04 This was called clipping, and you could be put to death for it.
L05 So in sixteen sixty-two, England began making coins by machine, with a pattern on the edge.
L06 Now a shaved coin was easy to spot!
L07 But old smooth coins were still everywhere. So in sixteen ninety-six, England remade all its money.
L08 And the man hunting the cheaters was Isaac Newton!
L09 Yes, the gravity guy.
L10 His biggest target was a forger named William Chaloner, who even faked the edges.
L11 Newton questioned the witnesses himself, and Chaloner was hanged.
L12 Today's coins are not silver. But the ridges stayed, and they help blind people tell coins apart by touch!
L13 So those ridges are a leftover from when people shaved their money!
