Hook (owner chose option A + razor gag, 2026-11-25): frame 1 = extreme close-up of a quarter's ridged edge, then a
thief's hand. L02-L03 play "shave" literally (shaving cream + razor) and correct it; real method (snipping slivers)

L01 The tiny ridges on your coins were put there to stop thieves!
L02 Because coins used to be made of real silver, and people would shave them.
L03 Well, not with shaving cream.
L04 They snipped a thin sliver off the edge. Do that to a hundred coins, melt the slivers, and you have free silver!
L05 This was called clipping, and you could be put to death for it.
L06 So in sixteen sixty-two, England began making coins by machine, with a pattern on the edge.
L07 Now a clipped coin was easy to spot!
L08 But old smooth coins were still everywhere. So in sixteen ninety-six, England remade all its money.
L09 And the man hunting the cheaters was Isaac Newton!
L10 Yes, the gravity guy.
L11 His biggest target was a forger named William Chaloner, who even faked the edges.
L12 Newton questioned the witnesses himself, and Chaloner was hanged.
L13 Today's coins are not silver. But the ridges stayed, and they help blind people tell coins apart by touch!
L14 So every time you feel those ridges, you are touching an anti-theft trick over three hundred years old!
