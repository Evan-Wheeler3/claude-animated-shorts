L01 People used to *shave* the edges off their coins, and then spend them *anyway*!
L02 Back then, a silver coin was worth the silver *inside* it.
L03 So shave a little off a hundred coins, melt the shavings, and you have *free* silver!
L04 This was called clipping, and you could be put to *death* for it.
L05 So in sixteen sixty-two, England started making coins by machine, with a pattern pressed into the *edge*.
L06 Now, if someone shaved a coin, the missing edge gave them *away*!
L07 But the old, smooth coins were still *everywhere*.
L08 So in sixteen ninety-six, England melted down its old money and made it *all* again.
L09 And the man whose job was to catch the cheaters was *Isaac* *Newton*!
L10 Yes, the *gravity* guy.
L11 His biggest target was William Chaloner, a forger who had even learned to *fake* the edges.
L12 Newton questioned the witnesses himself, and Chaloner was *hanged*.
L13 Today's coins are not silver. So why keep the ridges?
L14 Partly tradition. But the ridges also help *blind* people tell coins apart by *touch*!
L15 So those ridges are a leftover from when people *shaved* their money!
