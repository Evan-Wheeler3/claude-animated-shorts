# Why Guardrails Crumple — Shot list

49-second edit target. Align the cuts to the recorded read. Hold the old-cap impact itself for about a second. Give the mechanism reveal about six seconds so viewers can follow the steel through the head.

| # | Time | Picture and action | Narration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00–0:04 | Open mid-skid on a wide shot that shows the bend and the drop. The car zips off the cliff. The driver is still turning the wheel in midair. | “The road was near a cliff. One bad turn could send you right over the edge.” |
| 2 | 0:04–0:07 | Snap back to the same wide shot. Guardrail posts pop into place along the edge, followed by the steel beam. | “So the city built guardrails.” |
| 3 | 0:07–0:12 | Replay the skid. Track alongside as the car glances off the rail, flexes it, and straightens out along the road. | “Now, if a car slid into one from the side, the rail could guide it back along the road.” |
| 4 | 0:12–0:18 | Cut low and close. Replay just the flex at the point where the side of the car contacts the beam. | “But the rail had a problem. It could bend when you hit it from the side.” |
| 5 | 0:18–0:24 | Cut to bumper height, looking along the old rail toward the approaching car. Time the hit to “sudden stop.” The bumper buckles and the car stops sharply. | “Hit the old end cap straight on, though, and your car came to a sudden stop.” |
| 6 | 0:24–0:27 | Cut inside on impact. The seat belt snaps taut and the driver jolts forward. | “That’s what made it so deadly.” |
| 7 | 0:27–0:32 | Return to the bumper-height angle. Swap in the new impact head and push in so its broad face is easy to read. | “So the city put in new rails. These had a mechanism right at the end.” |
| 8 | 0:32–0:37 | Replay the head-on hit. Track beside the bumper as it pushes the head down the beam. Keep the fixed posts in view so the movement is clear. | “When the bumper hits it, the head slides down the rail.” |
| 9 | 0:37–0:43 | Cut away part of the head. In one continuous shot, show the wavy beam feeding in, getting squeezed flat, and curling out sideways as a strip. | “The wavy steel goes in one side, gets squeezed flat, and curls out the other.” |
| 10 | 0:43–0:49 | Pull wide while the car is still moving. Let it slow to a stop with the strip of steel beside it. The driver opens one eye. Cut there. | “It takes energy to bend all that steel. That gives the car more distance to slow down before it stops.” |
