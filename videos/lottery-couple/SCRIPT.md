# The Retired Couple Who Cracked The Lottery: script v2 (person beats a system, for the script gate)

Narrator voice B. ~180 words (~58 s; traffic-lights ran 3.1 words/s). Experiment (LEARNING-LOG.md): **visual simplicity** (ANIMATION-PLAYBOOK.md 1b):
one subject per shot, plain bright sets, a new picture every 2–2.5 s. Story shape from the two reference Shorts:
person + stunt in line 1 → why it works → a setback → the fix → trouble → a huge number → a closing image.
Title options: "He Cracked The Lottery In 2 Minutes" · "The Retired Couple Who Beat The Lottery" · "The Lottery Had A Math Mistake"

L01 A retired shop owner figured out how to beat the lottery in under two minutes.
L02 Normally, if nobody matches all six numbers, the jackpot just keeps growing.
L03 But in a game called WinFall, once the jackpot got big and nobody won, it was split between everyone who matched just a few numbers.
L04 And matching a few numbers is easy, if you buy enough tickets.
L05 Jerry did the math: those weeks, every hundred dollars of tickets paid back about a hundred and twenty.
L06 So he bet twenty-two hundred dollars... and lost fifty.
L07 He hadn't bought enough tickets. So he bet eight thousand, and won almost sixteen thousand.
L08 He told his wife Marge, and she was in.
L09 Friends and family chipped in, and they bought hundreds of thousands of tickets.
L10 Then a newspaper spotted stores selling millions in tickets, and the state suspected organized crime.
L11 Investigators found a retired couple... doing math. And it was all legal.
L12 In the end, their group won nearly twenty-seven million dollars.
L13 *(pause, ~0.4 s)* And they kept every ticket in tubs in their barn, in case of an audit.

## Changes v1 -> v2 (owner: "the story is a bit hard to follow")
- The trick is now explained in plain steps a viewer can picture: normal jackpot keeps growing (L02) → WinFall hands it to
  small winners instead (L03) → small wins are easy with lots of tickets (L04) → one concrete ratio, $100 in, ~$120 back (L05),
  like the pudding Short's "every quarter = 100 miles".
- Cut side trips that added names and places without moving the story: the brochure, the company name, Michigan ->
  Massachusetts and the 14-hour drive, the profit figure. One number per beat.
- Marge gets her own short line (L08) so the "couple" is clear.
