# Phil Won’t Stop Using Your Bathroom

**Story ID** `phil-bathroom`  
**Format** Fictional comedy short, vertical 9:16, 30 fps, 44.7-second target  
**Style** Absurd 3D Comedy, early PC-game look. Approved visual benchmark is the rendered bathroom reveal.  
**Stage** Full script and shot plan drafted for production review. The owner approved the look of the rendered bathroom reveal. Its motion proof provides the timing reference.

## Story script

Your neighbor Phil uses your bathroom like it came with his house. He brings the newspaper. He leaves the toilet unflushed. A deadbolt sends him to the side window. Bars send him back with a file. You board the window. Now the house looks like it’s from Detroit, and Phil drives through the garage.

This madness needs to stop. You buy a highly trained guard dog. It turns out to be a tiny pug. Phil offers a rotisserie chicken and wins him over immediately. At some point in this transaction, the dog becomes Gregory.

You put up a twelve-foot concrete wall. Phil pole-vaulted in high school. He vaults it with the concentration of a man stepping off a curb. You spend your life savings upgrading the house to a fortress. Steel doors. Laser sensors. Guards. The homeowner finally thinks he’s won.

He sinks into his bubble bath. The camera slides right. Phil is already on the neighboring toilet, reading the newspaper. The homeowner looks at him. Phil glances back. One small fart. Cut to black.

## What sells it

Phil thinks he’s carrying out a perfectly ordinary errand. The homeowner takes every setback personally. Each upgrade grows out of the previous failure, and no one ever explains why Phil is so committed to this bathroom. The newspaper ties his first appearance to the final reveal.

## Cast and geography

- **Phil** has the seated and standing forms in the supplied pack. Preserve the face, olive shirt, sneakers, and matter-of-fact posture across the short. The newspaper is his recurring prop.
- **The homeowner** is the viewer’s stand-in. Match the supplied bath likeness for the clothed exterior and hallway version. Show stress through his grip, neck tension and posture, then let it go just before the reveal.
- **Gregory** is the apartment-sized pug invited to do the job of a full security team. Two uniformed guards can share one rig and wardrobe toward the end.
- **One suburban house** has a front door, a clearly different side window, an attached garage, a short side path that ends at the yard wall, and the tiled bathroom inside. Use cuts and light changes to imply the passage of time.
