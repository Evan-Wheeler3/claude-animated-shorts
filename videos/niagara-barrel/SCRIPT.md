# Hold Up — Annie Edson Taylor's Niagara Falls Barrel
Target ~36–42 seconds, ~135 words, narration starts immediately. Narration only, no captions.

## Narration (approved for production as draft; adjust pacing only)
In 1901, a 63-year-old woman decided to go over Niagara Falls.

Inside a wooden barrel.

Her name was Annie Edson Taylor. And she had a plan to get rich.

First, she tested her barrel with a cat.

It came back alive.

So Annie climbed inside, strapped herself in, and had the lid sealed.

Then the current carried her straight toward the edge.

And down she went.

One hundred and sixty-seven feet.

When rescuers finally opened the barrel, Annie was alive.

She'd survived Niagara Falls with relatively minor injuries.

But there was one problem.

She'd done all this to make a fortune.

And she barely made any money.

Turns out surviving a waterfall is easier than getting rich from it.

## Fact notes
- Annie Edson Taylor went over Horseshoe Falls on October 24, 1901, her 63rd birthday, in a specially built barrel; she survived.
- She tested a barrel with a cat beforehand; historical retellings vary on the cat's precise experience. Do not depict the cat injured.
- The Horseshoe Falls drop is often described as ~167 ft; use this as approximate.
- Her financial ambitions largely failed; her barrel was taken by her manager at one point, and she earned little.
- Do not depict her falling freely outside the barrel or imply she deliberately opened the lid herself.
- Verify primary facts against Niagara Parks / Niagara Falls historical museum / Smithsonian before publishing. Avoid invented quotations or precise timings.
