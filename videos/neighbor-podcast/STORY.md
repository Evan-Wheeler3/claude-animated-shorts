## Premise and escalation

Your neighbor has turned your doorbell camera into a podcast studio and is already on episode forty-six. You smash the doorbell. He interviews your baby on the nursery camera. You lock the cameras in a safe. He interviews the locksmith. You move out to the woods and finally get three quiet weeks. The next episode comes from your trail camera, with a bear in headphones.

## Cast and look

The neighbor hosts every episode with the same composure and the same adult folding chair. The homeowner takes each setback personally. The porch guest, baby, locksmith and bear each get one unmistakable silhouette and a distinct reaction.

Use Phil Bathroom's Absurd 3D Comedy direction as the episode reference. Stage the physical gags with readable hand contacts, restrained faces, warm practical lights and grounded silhouettes. Use the existing Three.js and Remotion workflow. The pinned style reference is [Phil's episode guide](https://github.com/Evan-Wheeler3/claude-animated-shorts/blob/c455d9a7917d4efe7b9bd274ba654a0f23b40a0c/videos/phil-bathroom/ANIMATION-STYLE.md).

## Editorial constraints

- English, vertical, 30 fps, 51-second target and 60-second ceiling. Final target 1440 × 2560.
- Start on the doorbell feed. Use practical sound and device audio to distinguish camera views. No captions, title card or outro.
- Record narrator and neighbor separately. The homeowner and guests have no spoken lines. Keep all spoken cues disjoint.
- At 0:17 the baby straightens up and triggers a brief monitor-filtered crowd cheer. The cheer has its own beat.
- The bear leans into the microphone, takes one breath, and the video cuts to black before it answers.
